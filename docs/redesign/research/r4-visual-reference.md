# Visual reference study: edges, streaks, metal, touch, industrial, micro detail

How the study was done: I looked at about 75 photographs in the built-in browser. Most came
from Google Images previews at about 300 px, plus one Wikimedia Commons search. The sources
are mostly Japanese repair and painting contractors' blogs, US concrete-repair pages, Reddit,
and a few stock thumbnails; I skipped stock images that looked AI-generated. Each search URL
below is the results page I worked from. Counts are from the photos I saw myself, so treat
them as rough proportions. Sizes are estimated against steps (about 18 cm), sashes and rail
posts (about 1.1 m).

## 1. Chipped arrises and corners

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=chipped+concrete+corner+edge+spalling+close+up&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=コンクリート+角+欠け+写真&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=階段+段鼻+欠け+コンクリート&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=chipped+concrete+curb+edge+damage+photo&udm=2.
- Commons search: https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/index.php?search=concrete+spalling&title=Special:MediaSearch&type=image.
- 19 breaks: steps (Sakrete, DIYおやじ), planters and a parking slab (事前見積り), a gutter block, curbs (Quikrete, TruSeal), a sidewalk, Jersey barriers (NYSDOT), a column base (Eng-Tips), and a deck edge and wall spall (Commons).

**What I saw:**
- **Runs, not divots.** In 9 of 19 the damage is a run of overlapping bites, each 0.5–3 cm,
  along 20–60 cm of edge. Long stretches between runs are intact, with one run per element,
  occasionally two.
- **Shell breaks.** One larger conchoidal "shell" break appears in about 6 of 19. Each is
  10–40 cm long and 3–8 cm deep, deepest at the arris, feathering into one face.
- **One face loses.**
  - On mid-edge breaks, one face clearly loses most in about 12 of 15.
  - On a horizontal top arris, the top loses a sliver of 1–3 cm and the vertical face takes
    the taller scoop.
  - Real two-face removal showed up only at vertices, as a single wedge, in 3 of 19.
  - I never saw two big divots flanking one edge.
- **Where.**
  - Vertices, or within 15 cm of one: 7 of 19.
  - Joints: 4 of 19.
  - On vertical arrises, damage sat in the 0–1.2 m impact band.
  - Rebar-cover spalls are linear, 5–15 cm tall along the bar line, often on the soffit just
    behind a slab edge (Commons, ベストウイング).
- **Colour.**
  - Fresh breaks are paler, with rough exposed aggregate.
  - Old breaks are rounded and dark, and hold dirt.
  - A mortar topping comes off as a flat-backed plate 2–3 cm thick.
- **Whole-arris wear.** On old curbs and steps the entire arris is rounded by 5–15 mm, and
  the breaks sit inside that rounding.

**Rules:**
1. Two scales:
   - a noisy wear radius along the whole edge, 3–15 mm;
   - 0–2 runs per edge, each 20–60 cm of power-law bites (0.5–3 cm) plus at most one shell
     break (10–40 cm).
2. **One-sided.** Carve the struck face: the vertical face on curbs, steps and copings, or
   the outward face at corners. Give the other face at most 1/3 of that depth. Allow a
   wedge only at vertices, and never place two large removals opposite each other.
3. **Shell section.** Depth is 0.3–0.5 of the length along the edge, and the facets are
   rough, never planar.
4. Placement weights: vertices ×3, joints ×2, vertical arrises at 0–1.2 m ×3, above 2.5 m
   ×0.2, except cover spalls at slab edges.
5. **Substrate.** Fresh breaks are pale and grainy; aged ones are grimy with a rounded lip.
   Painted concrete shows grey concrete and a thin paint rim.

## 2. Weathered facades, Japanese apartment blocks

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=団地+外壁+雨だれ+汚れ&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=マンション+ベランダ+外壁+雨だれ+筋&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=古い団地+外壁+劣化&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=old+danchi+japan+apartment+block+weathered+concrete+facade+balcony&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=エアコン+配管カバー+外壁+汚れ+筋&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=concrete+apartment+balcony+slab+edge+water+staining+streaks+below&udm=2.
- 23 photos: ミヤケン, しろくまペイント, キリンテック, SMSJapan and other contractors, plus Shutterstock (an abandoned danchi) and Palm Build.

**Origins.** In 17 of 17 streak photos, every streak starts at an edge or an attachment:
- sill ends and lower window corners: 6, from both ends of the sill, not the middle;
- horizontal tops with no drip (parapets, a gable top, a retaining wall under a coping,
  inner parapet faces): 6;
- attachments (vent hoods, grille posts, a bracket, rail posts, a duct cover): 5.

**Shapes.**
- **Sill-end streak.** 3–8 cm wide at the origin. It fans out at 10–20° to 15–25 cm and is
  darkest in the first 10–30 cm. Most end at 0.5–1.5 m. Two ends make a V-fan, and the wall
  under the middle of the sill stays cleaner.
- **Top-edge fringe.** A 5–15 cm dark band under the lip, then many streaks 0.5–3 cm wide,
  spaced irregularly 2–30 cm apart. Most stop at 20–40 % of the face height, and a few reach
  the bottom.
- **Inner faces of balcony and corridor parapets.** A hair-fine fringe every 1–3 cm over the
  full ~1 m height (SMSJapan, タケ・ホープ). Outer faces with a drip lip were cleaner.
- **Vertical attachments.** A dark line down each side edge. A removed duct cover left a
  clean ghost strip with dirty margins: the vertical, wind-driven case.
- **Rail posts on a slab edge,** about every 1.2 m. Each has a spall at the post pocket and a
  rust streak down the full slab face, with efflorescence drips between (Palm Build).

**At 30–60 m** the streaks sort into columns that line up with the openings. The walls
between the openings, and the building corners, are paler, and the parapet carries a dark
band. Some window columns are much worse than others: about 1–2 in 5.

**Pale washed streaks** I saw clearly in only 2 photos, as a late-life feature. For the
base I saw only one clear example: a paint-peel band of 10–20 cm at a corridor floor.

**Rules:**
1. Spawn streaks only from features: sill ends, opening corners, tops without a drip,
   fixings, post bases, and the lower lip of a chip. Seed each source's strength so that
   20–30 % of them dominate.
2. Profile: full strength at the origin, widening at 10–20° and fading exponentially.
   Lengths follow a power law (median 0.3–0.5 m, a 5 % tail of 2–3 m).
3. Use a fringe only on a horizontal top that has no drip, including the inner faces of
   parapets. Faces below a modelled drip groove stay nearly clean.
4. Vertical attachments and corners get edge lines, not centre streaks. Outer corners read
   washed and paler.
5. Vary strength per column of openings, through several floors, not per bay.

## 3. Painted steel

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=外壁+もらい錆+手すり+根元+錆汁&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=鉄部+塗装+剥がれ+錆+手すり+集合住宅&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=鉄扉+下部+錆+塗装剥がれ+団地&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=シャッター+下部+錆+腐食+倉庫&udm=2.
- 22 photos: ミヤケン, 丸巧, 桜井市の屋根工事, 関東シャッターセンター and others.

**What I saw:**
- **Railings.** 9 of 10 rusted worst in three places, with a run below each junction. The
  vertical balusters were mostly sound.
  - the top surfaces of horizontal rails;
  - where balusters meet a rail;
  - post bases, with a black and rust halo of 5–10 cm on the concrete.
- **Doors.**
  - The worst is in the bottom 10–30 cm and along the lock or hinge edge.
  - The paint comes off as crisp curled flakes, in layers: topcoat, then older light paint,
    then rust.
  - The large faces chalk and fade evenly (an orange danchi door faded to tan).
- **Shutters.** Rust runs as horizontal streaks along one to three slat interlocks, not all
  of them. The bottom rail and the bottom 20–30 cm of the guides are worst.
- **Rust runs.** 1–4 cm wide at the source and 0.3–1.5 m long. They go burnt orange to
  brown-grey, and on corrugated sheet they follow the valleys with teardrop ends.

**Rules:**
1. Failure mask = upward-facing horizontal members + junction proximity (8–15 cm) + the
   bottom 0.3 m + door bottoms and lock edges. Elsewhere, only chalk.
2. Each spot drives a bloom, a blister halo 1.5–2× its size, and a run.
3. Peel as crisp-edged flakes over a lighter older layer, not as a soft gradient.

## 4. Human touch points

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=worn+painted+handrail+paint+worn+off+by+hands&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=dirty+door+around+handle+grime+hand+marks+painted+door&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=dirty+wall+around+light+switch+hand+marks&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=old+elevator+buttons+worn+numbers+rubbed+off+stainless+panel&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=worn+stone+steps+dished+centre+footfall+threshold&udm=2.
- About 18 photos, mostly from Reddit and Facebook, plus KHIA/Belzona and Pond5.

**What I saw:**
- **Doors.** Grime is not a disc round the handle. On 4 of 5 doors it is a vertical band on
  the latch edge, 10–20 cm wide at about 0.8–1.7 m. The door's edge face is darkest, and
  there is more grime above the handle than below.
- **Switches.** The grime is offset toward the side people approach from, at 1.5–3× the
  plate size (2 of 3).
- **Handrails.**
  - The paint is worn through in a ragged strip 2–4 cm wide on the top and grip side.
  - It is worst mid-flight and on the starting newel cap.
  - The underside is intact.
- **Lifts.** One or two buttons (ground floor, door-open) are worn far more than the rest.
  Brass is polished at the centre, with patina left at the rim.
- **Stairs.**
  - Wear sits off-centre: beside the handrail, or on the inside of a turn.
  - Dishing is in the front half of the tread.
  - The sides stay sharp, with moss where nobody steps.

**Rules:**
1. Give touch targets power-law usage weights, so that one or two hot targets take most of
   the wear.
2. Shapes: an edge band (doors), an offset fan (switches), a top strip (rails), a centre
   polish (buttons), a front-half dish (treads).
3. Grime on paint is darker and slightly glossier; polish on metal is brighter and smoother.

## 5. Industrial

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=warehouse+concrete+column+forklift+damage+corner+paint+scuffs&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=warehouse+floor+forklift+tire+marks+worn+aisle+line+paint&udm=2, https://www.google.com/search?q=loading+dock+edge+worn+yellow+paint+bumpers+tire+marks&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=pallet+rack+upright+damaged+forklift+dent+scraped+paint+base&udm=2.
- About 10 photos; many results were marketing shots of new floors, which I set aside.

**What I saw:**
- **Columns.**
  - At an aisle column end (Ramp Champ), black tyre scuffs cluster at 0.3–1.2 m within
    about 40 cm of the arris.
  - They are directional slashes radiating from impact points.
  - The arris is chipped in the same band.
- **Rack uprights.** Only the aisle-facing front upright is hit, at 0.1–0.6 m: a bent flange
  and paint scraped to bare steel on the flange edge. Only a few uprights in a row are hit.
- **Floors.** Old floors are mottled with trowel marks and darker tyre lanes.
- **Dock bumpers.** Timber bumpers are splintered at the top-front corner, with debris
  heaped at their feet.

**Rules:**
1. Band damage by vehicle height (0–1.2 m on columns, 0–0.6 m on uprights), on the aisle
   side and near the arris.
2. Scuffs are anisotropic strokes along the direction of travel.
3. Use a random subset of 1 in 4–8 elements, with one hero.

## 6. Surface micro detail

**Sources:**
- Searches: https://www.google.com/search?q=concrete+bug+holes+surface+voids+formed+wall+top+of+pour&udm=2 and https://www.google.com/search?q=吹付けタイル+外壁+劣化+アップ&udm=2.
- About 13 photos, including a 40-year danchi wall texture (ぱくたそ).

**What I saw:**
- **Bug holes cluster.**
  - On the board-formed wall (Reddit) they are about 10× denser in the top 30–40 cm of the
    pour than lower down.
  - They are denser in the upper half of each board band.
  - Concrete Surface Lab shows patches of holes next to nearly bare areas.
  - Sizes run from 2 to 20 mm, mostly small.
- **Spray-tile texture.**
  - The flattened (押さえ) type is flat-topped mesas, 5–20 mm across, with crisp rims and
    troughs between. The other (凸凹) type is rounded bumps.
  - The 40-year wall shows patchy tone, dark specks and scratches, not even grain.
- **Dirt follows texture.** Dirt lodges in the troughs, so streaks read stronger on rough
  texture.

**Rules:**
1. Modulate micro density by construction: dense near the top of each pour lift, near
   edges, and in per-panel patches. Give it a spread of 5–10× between areas.
2. Spray-tile height = flattened, thresholded mesas plus a small rounded noise. Dirt and AO
   go in the troughs; chalk and polish go on the plateaus.
3. Upward faces get dust. Soffits stay uniformly greyer, because no rain cleans them.

## Mismatches in our renders, ranked by how much they give away CG

1. **Chips are isolated, planar-faceted divots.**
   - In cmp_block_close (ii)/(iii) two large triangular scoops sit side by side on the face,
     with flat facets and the same albedo as the face.
   - In 12_damage_corner small dark rectangular notches are evenly spaced up the full arris
     and read like decals. The base, where impacts cluster, is untouched.
   - What the photos show is one-sided runs plus at most one shell break, concentrated at
     0–1.2 m, with pale or grimy substrate.
2. **Streaks are distributed bands, not sourced.**
   - In 03 and 05, soft vertical bands of similar width and length hang under every coping,
     and read as the same hatch on every floor at 45 m.
   - None comes from a sill end, rail post or AC bracket. There is no V-fan, no widening,
     and no dark band under the lip.
3. **The micro texture is uniform.** In 02 and cmp_wall_close the same rounded-bump noise
   covers face, coping top and soffit. There are no mesas, no dirt in the troughs, no dust
   on tops, and no density change by panel or pour.
4. **The metal tells no water story.** Rails, partition frames and AC brackets are clean or
   carry small orange dabs. Nothing marks the junctions, horizontal tops or post bases, and
   no rust runs onto the slab edge below each post.
5. **There is no touch layer.** Doors, partitions and rails show no latch-edge band, no
   rail-top wear, and no wear concentrated near the entrance.
6. **The rust streak in cmp_block_close (ii)** starts at the chip lip, which is correct, but
   it is a constant-width red line about 1 m long. It should be orange at the source, widen,
   fade, and be shorter.
7. **Edges are uniformly crisp and clean,** with no whole-arris wear radius and no paler
   washed corners.
8. **The base junction is clean** (12, 05): no splash band, debris line or peel band. The
   evidence for this was thin.
9. **Floors repeat** (05). Real variation runs by column of openings and by a few neglected
   flats.
