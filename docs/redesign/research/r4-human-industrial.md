# Human contact, traffic and industrial wear: research for the skills

[S] = sourced (numbered below); [I] = inference. Extends, without repeating, `believability.md`,
`realism.md` and `techniques.md`.

**Main idea:** codes fix the heights of hardware, rails, buttons and slots, so wear heights come from the
codes, not guesses. The protection products (kick plates, rack guards, crash rails) mark where damage lands.

**Material response** (one hand-contact field; each material reads it differently):

| Material | Change at touch points |
|---|---|
| Unlacquered brass/bronze | patina visible after "just 10 days of normal use" [S15]; rubbed zones stay gold-bright (roughness 0.2–0.3), recesses go dark brown [I] |
| Lacquered brass | lacquer wears through: bright patch, tarnished ring at the lacquer edge [I] |
| Anodised aluminium | dye and matte layer abrade: paler, roughness −0.15–0.25 [I; S16 weak] |
| Painted steel | paint → primer → bare steel; **constantly handled steel stays dark-polished, rarely touched exposures rust** [I] |
| Varnished wood | "roughness increased greatly at the spots that were touched the most" [S17]; gloss dulls, matte shines, both toward 0.35–0.5 [I] |
| Wall paint, stainless | skin oil holds dirt: albedo −10–25 %; fingerprint grease on stainless [I] |

**Variation** [I]: door swing and traffic rank (§5) matter more than handedness, since the door fixes the
handle.

## 2. Foot and wheel traffic

- **Desire lines.** Helbing: trails fade without use, attract walkers and **bundle** toward a "minimal
  detour system", settling at a compromise between directness and reuse [S18].
- **Floor lanes.** "Approximately 42% of a floor's finish can be removed within the first six feet" of
  an entrance; 70–80 % of dirt enters through doors (WAXIE [S19]). Cleaners apply "one coat to
  non-trafficked edges for every three" in lanes [S21]. **So lanes go dull and scuffed; edges go glossy,
  yellowed and grimy, with a dark line at the skirting.**
- **Stairs.** The nosing wear point is "only 20mm – 25mm" deep on the tread, plus about 10 mm down the
  face, centred; descending feet land at the front edge, the tread proper is contacted mostly going up
  [S22]. Old stairs wear more in the centre than at the ends [S23]. [I]: lane 40–60 % of the width, shifted
  toward the rail; two lanes above about 1.8 m wide; dish 1–5 mm (concrete) to 10–40 mm (old stone). A
  parish threshold took 119 years to dip until unsafe [S24]; thresholds dish toward the latch side [I].
- **Low marks** (heights from protection products): kicks 0–250 mm and mops 0–150 mm [S6]; chair backs
  0.7–0.9 m, hence chair rails at 26–36 in [S25]; trolley strikes on 200 mm crash-rail bands in hospital
  corridors [S26].
- **Cleaning** [I]: faint figure-8 mop arcs; 50–150 mm grime triangles in corners; unworn outlines under
  moved furniture.
- **Gum**: 78 % of dropped litter items; 22 pieces in one square foot in Milton Keynes; hotspots at bus
  stops, stations and shop parades [S27]. [I]: black discs 10–25 mm, densest within 2–5 m of stops and
  doors, on the kerb half of the footway.
- **Cigarettes**: 30 % of litter at "transition points" [S28]; most at bars and transit stops [S29]. [I]:
  5–10 mm burns on bench slats and ledges near entrances; stub-out smears on walls at 1.0–1.5 m.

## 3. Residential and urban marks

- **Graffiti.** Writers "choose locations that optimize exposure" (12,655 instances, Ghent); high tags
  carry farther but are cleaned faster [S30]. [I]: tags mostly 0.8–2.2 m on shutters, doors, utility
  boxes and sign backs; mismatched buff rectangles over old tags, with new tags on the buff.
- **Stickers** [I]: 1.2–1.8 m on poles and boxes; torn residue is white paper fibre plus a dirt-trapping
  adhesive outline, in layered generations.
- **Repairs** [I]: tape on cracked glass; A4 notices at 1.3–1.7 m, one corner lifting; filler patches in a
  near-match colour.
- **Bikes** [I]: tyre rubber 0–0.7 m, pedal gouges 0.2–0.4 m, bar scuffs 0.9–1.1 m, in a 1.8 m cluster.
- **Pots** [I]: rust or salt rings the size of the base, 2–3 overlapping.
- **AC streaks** start at drain outlets of indoor heads, not under every outdoor unit [S31][S32].

| Dock door tracks | base 2–3 ft is "the most frequently forklift-impacted part" [S37] | mast and counterweight strikes [S38]; bollards 42 in, 1000 mm at docks [S39] |
| Roller shutters | mast strikes the rolled door [S40] | [I]: dents at 0.1–0.6 m and 0.8–1.6 m (loads); odd-colour replaced bottom slats |
| Tyre marks | worst with solid tyres: heat deposits rubber in the pores [S41] | grooves near racking, docks and turning radii [S42]; [I] black arcs at aisle ends, fading 5–15 m past turns |
| Fork drag | long gouges of varying depth [S43] | pallet scratches along pick faces [S44] |
| Floor joints | damage worst with "small hard vehicle wheels", crossing joints at 90° [S45]; it "will only get worse" [S45] | [I]: arris spalls 10–50 mm each side, 5–20 mm deep, concentrated where aisles cross joints |
| Floor markings | lines 2–6 in [S46]; tape not for "steering, braking and turning" [S47] | [I]: lines survive in straight runs and fail at crossings, turns and dock aprons |
| Oil, hydraulics | drip trays go under parked machines; polished concrete absorbs oil [S48] | [I]: pools 0.2–0.6 m under the engine or cylinder, darkest at the centre, a soft halo, tyre-width tracking 3–10 m |
| Battery charging | acid pitting "up to 40cm deep" in the worst case [S49] | [I]: etched, lighter, exposed aggregate; white or yellow crust |
| Heat | stainless tint: pale yellow 290 °C, straw 340, brown 390, purple 450, blue 540, dark blue 600 °C [S50] | [I]: bands around flanges and exhausts; soot plume above flues, widening upward |
| Galvanising | white rust is a "bulky white or gray deposit" where moisture sits unventilated [S51] | weathered zinc "seldom attacked" [S51]; [I] white rust only in crevices and overlaps |
| Column bases | dust and moisture collect at base plates [S52] | [I]: rust at the grout line in the bottom 0–150 mm, worst on the wet side |
| Dirt gradient | 70–80 % enters at doors [S19] | [I]: dirt density falls exponentially, halving every 5–15 m from the dock door, raised again in lanes |

**Post-industrial decay** (water drives it; masonry outlasts roofs and floors [S53]; times [I]):
**0–2 years:** blocked gutters, leaks under roof penetrations, ground-level vandalism (glass 0–3 m,
stripped copper, opened panels). **2–10:** ponding, rust runs, blown paint, soft timber, moss, guano under
openings. **10–30:** roof sheets lost, purlins and floors fail below leaks, trees in gutters.

## 5. Distribution: 80/20 for use

- **Touch is concentrated.** Only five hospital-room surfaces (bed rails, bed, supply cart, over-bed
  table, IV pump) qualified as high-touch [S54]. Rank a building the same way [I]: main entrance > core
  doors > handrail ends > unit doors > service doors.
- **Traffic funnels**: bundling [S18] and the 6-foot entrance figure [S19] put wear at entrances, pinch
  points, turns and stair starts.
- **Infer from function** [I]: every operable part is a touch point at its code height; every opening has
  a push side, a pull side and a foot zone; every door, stair, lift or dock is a path endpoint; vertical
  edges within 0.6 m of a turn take strikes at the mover's bumper height.
- **Store** [I]:
  - **per piece**, an `interaction` point cloud authored with it: `P`, `N`, `kind`
    (grip/push/press/slot/foot/strike), `radius`, `aniso`, `side`, `use_weight`. It rides the instance
    transform.
  - **per building**, a path graph (entrances → cores → units, or navmesh shortest paths) accumulated
    into floor `traffic`, blurred 0.3–1 m, raised to γ 2–3 to bundle, normalised per level.
  - `touch = use_weight × traffic_near × rank`; hot = top 15–25 %, the rest 10–30 %.
  - `strike = traffic × convexity × (1 − dist/d)`, d = 0.3 m for feet and carts, 0.6–1.0 m for forklifts.

## 6. Procedural rules

**Geometry** [I unless marked]

| Damage | Rule |
|---|---|
| Worn tread | erosion depth `d = dmax · lane(x)^1.5 · nose(z)`. `lane` is a Gaussian centred 0.45–0.55 of the width (σ 0.2 W), shifted toward the rail; `nose` is 1 for the first 25 mm, fading over 60 mm [S22]. Round the nosing r 3–15 mm; dmax 1–5 mm (concrete) to 10–40 mm (old stone) |
| Kick and cart scuffs | below 1 mm, normal map only; dents 2–8 mm deep, r 20–60 mm, at 0.1–0.3 m and 0.3–1.0 m on push-side door and frame corners |
| Forklift strikes | rack upright bends of 3–15 mm over 0.1–0.3 m. Column guard and bollard dents r 50–150 mm at 0.1–0.5 m. Wall and kerb corners chipped 20–80 mm at 0–0.5 m; door track bends at 0–0.9 m |
| Shutter dents | r 100–300 mm, 5–30 mm deep, at 0.1–0.6 m and 0.8–1.6 m, crossing slat seams |
| Floor joint arris | spall pockets 10–50 mm wide, 5–20 mm deep along the joint; density × aisle traffic |
| Fork gouges | V grooves 2–5 mm wide, 0.3–2 m long, parallel to the aisle, near pick faces |

**Texture and shader layers** (all read `touch`, `traffic`, `strike`)

| Layer | Mask | Albedo | Roughness | Metal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grip polish (metal) | `touch`, tight (r 30–80 mm), anisotropic along the grip | brass +15–30 % toward gold; steel neutral | −0.2–0.35 | as base |
| Grease (painted, stone, wall) | `touch`, wider halo (1.5–3× the polish radius) | −10–25 %, toward warm grey-brown | −0.05–0.15 | 0 |
| Paint → primer → metal | `touch` or `strike` thresholds 0.5 / 0.75 / 0.9 | primer red-oxide (≈0.20, 0.07, 0.04 linear) or grey; bare steel ≈0.55 | primer 0.7; steel 0.3 if `touch`, rust if `strike` with damp | 1 on bare steel |
| Floor lane | `traffic` | finish worn: +3–8 % value; black scuffs at turns | +0.1 on gloss floors | 0 |
| Floor edge | `1 − traffic` near walls | yellowed, darker within 0.3 m of the skirting | −0.1 (finish build-up) | 0 |
| Tyre marks | `traffic` × curvature of the path (turns) | near-black rubber, 0.03–0.05 linear, 30–60 % opacity | −0.05 | 0 |
| Oil | parked-machine points | −30–50 % on concrete, soft halo, tracked streaks | −0.2–0.4 when fresh | 0 |
| Heat tint | distance from the source | the S50 sequence by distance | as metal | 1 |

**Checks:** touch layers sit at code heights (§1); hot points hold 70–85 % of each layer; no contact wear
where nothing is operable or nobody walks.
